The Five Garage Door Accessories That Actually Sell

Replacement gear kits, copy remotes, decorative hardware, universal receivers and Wi-Fi control. What each accessory is for, who buys it, and how to attach it to an opener order.

An opener is a one-off sale. Accessories are the repeat business. A distributor who stocks openers but not the five categories below ends up sending the customer to a marketplace for every lost remote and every worn gear — and loses the margin twice.

Here is what each of the Autogatelink accessory lines is actually for.

1. 41C4220A Gear — the repair that keeps old openers alive

What it is: a gear and sprocket kit, typically compatible with 1/3 HP and 1/2 HP chain-drive garage door openers. A replacement part.

Who buys it: anyone with an older chain-drive opener whose drive gear has stripped. This is the classic failure mode on chain-drive units — the door stops, the motor still runs, and the whole opener looks dead when the actual fault is one worn plastic gear.

Why it matters commercially: it converts a "your opener is finished, buy a new one" conversation into a low-cost repair. That builds loyalty, and it costs almost nothing to hold in stock. Sell it as a kit, not as a loose part, and include it in the spares recommendation with every chain-drive opener you ship.

2. 2630AIO — the universal copy remote

What it is: a face-to-face copy duplicate multi-frequency universal garage door remote control.

ParameterValue
Operating voltageDC 6V
Operating current≤ 12 mA
Operation frequency303MHz / 310MHz / 315MHz / 433.92MHz / 868MHz, optional

Who buys it: the household that has lost a remote, or has one remote and three drivers. A face-to-face copy handset clones the signal from an existing working handset — no soldering, no opening the motor, no DIP switches to read.

Selling point: multi-frequency coverage from 303MHz up to 868MHz means one SKU serves most of the installed base in a market. That is the single most useful thing about a copy remote for a distributor: fewer SKUs, fewer stockouts. The ≤ 12 mA draw at DC 6V is what gives it a usable battery life in daily service.

One honest limitation to state up front: a copy remote reproduces the signal of an existing handset, so it is the wrong product for a system using rolling code security. Match it to fixed-code installations and you will have very few returns.

3. 2701DG — decorative hardware, the easiest upsell

What it is: decorative garage door hinges and handles. Faux garage door accents in metal, screw mounted, black, spear style. The kit contains 4 hinges and 2 handles.

Who buys it: the homeowner refreshing a plain steel sectional door without replacing it. Carriage-house style hardware changes the entire look of a facade for a fraction of the cost of a new door.

Selling point: it is screw mounted, so installation needs no modification to the door panel and no specialist tooling. It sells on photographs, which makes it ideal for a showroom display and for photo-heavy listings. Bundle it with a K8 Plus Pro opener and the whole garage reads as a premium installation.

4. 9102 — the universal receiver

What it is: a garage door remote control receiver, multi-frequency 300-900MHz, with transmitter, DIP switch coding and 2 channels.

ParameterValue
Channels2 CH
Working voltageAC/DC 9~30V
Frequency300MHz ~ 868MHz
ApplicationVilla, school, hotel, apartment, hospital, office

Who buys it: installers retrofitting an existing motor that has working mechanics but a dead or insecure radio board; and property managers who need to add a second channel for a pedestrian gate or a second door.

Selling point: the 9-30V AC/DC input range is what makes it genuinely universal — it will run off the control board of almost any motor on site without a separate supply. Two channels means one receiver can handle two doors, or a door plus a gate. The commercial application list is exactly where this sells: schools, hotels, apartment blocks and offices buy receivers in quantity, not one at a time.

5. 9103 — Wi-Fi control, the modernisation kit

What it is: a Wi-Fi garage door opener module for home automation, letting the door be controlled from an Android or iOS mobile device. Compatible with Alexa and Google Assistant.

Who buys it: the homeowner who wants to check the garage door from their phone and open it for a delivery, but has no intention of replacing a functioning motor.

Selling point: normally open / normally closed contacts with screw terminals mean it wires into an existing control board rather than replacing it. Wall mounted, plastic housing, push-button actuator — a straightforward retrofit. Voice control through Alexa and Google Assistant is now a standard expectation in residential projects, and this is the cheapest way to meet it.

How to attach accessories to the opener order

The pattern that works:

  • With every chain-drive opener: add a 41C4220A gear kit to the spares line. It is the part that will fail, and having it on the shelf removes a future complaint.
  • With every opener: quote two remotes minimum, plus a 2630AIO copy remote for the "we need one more" call.
  • With every premium install: add 2701DG hardware. It costs little, photographs well, and raises the perceived value of the whole job.
  • For retrofit and commercial: lead with the 9102 receiver. Two channels and a 9-30V input do most of the selling.
  • For residential upgrades: offer 9103 to every customer who asks about phone control.

All five lines ship alongside opener orders and are available for OEM and ODM projects. Tell us the target market and we will confirm frequency options and packaging.
